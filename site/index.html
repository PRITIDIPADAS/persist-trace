The tumour shrinks.
Some cancer cells survive.
What are they becoming?

Lung cancer cells with EGFR mutations can survive osimertinib without any new mutation by switching into a drug-tolerant persister state. PersistTrace takes 60 public RNA-seq experiments, removes the part of the signal that is just acute drug stress, measures what fades after the drug is stopped, and shows how strong the evidence is for every gene it flags.

Everything in this project, one swipe

01 / The question

Are persisters one population, or several states on different paths?

Most analyses compare treated cells with untreated cells. That mixes three different things: stress that every cell feels in the first day, the persister state that builds up over weeks, and recovery after the drug is gone. PersistTrace pulls those apart.

In plain words. Imagine a crowd hit by a storm. Everyone gets wet in the first hour, so "being wet" tells you nothing about who will still be standing after three weeks. We look for what changes only in the survivors, and for what goes back to normal when the storm stops.

Five conditions, four cell lines

PC9, NCI-H1975, HCC827 and HCC2935, each sequenced untreated, after 24 h of 500 nM osimertinib, as 21-day persisters, 24 h after washout and after regrowth (3 to 10 days, set per line by the original authors). Three biological replicates each.

What we add

A decomposition that strips the acute response from the persister signal, a population-level reversibility map with memory half-lives, and an evidence passport that keeps markers and mechanisms apart.

What we do not claim

No diagnosis, no treatment advice, no proof that any gene causes resistance. Cell lines are not patients. Every label on this page is an evidence level, and the passport says which level.

03 / Persistence-specific signal decomposition

Subtract the stress, keep the persister

For every gene and cell line we have the acute change A and the persister change P. A robust regression across all genes finds how much of the acute response persisters still carry. What is left, S, is the persister-specific part.

The idea in one line. If a gene goes up on day one and is still up on day 21, that is mostly stress. If it only goes up by day 21, that is the persister talking.

04 / Reversibility map

What does a cancer cell remember after the drug is gone?

Each persister change is followed into the 24 h washout and the regrowth washout. "Memory" is the fraction of the persister change still present. Where the GEO record gives the regrowth day, we fit an exponential decay and report a half-life.

Careful wording. These are averages over millions of cells. A bulk signal drifting back to baseline fits reversal, but it could also mean the persisters were outgrown by other cells. Only lineage tracing can tell those apart.

06 / The replication paradox

Asleep, or quietly copying DNA?

Persisters are often described as dormant. Lineage tracing in PC9 found a rare subset that keeps cycling under osimertinib, with its own antioxidant and fatty-acid programmes. Here we follow cell-cycle, DNA repair and metabolic programmes through the five conditions in each line.

Limit. Bulk RNA cannot show whether a few cells are cycling while most sleep. It shows the population average. The single-cell datasets listed in Methods are the next step.

07 / Biological Evidence Passport

Marker or mechanism? Six separate answers, no secret score

Each candidate gets six dimensions that are never added together. The final label is the strongest claim the evidence allows, picked by written rules. Contradictions are listed, not hidden.

Start with TROP2. It rises in persisters. A 2025 knockout study found it was not needed for persister formation. A 2026 study reported it helps persisters survive. Both can be true in different set-ups, and the passport shows exactly that.
08 / Validation arena

Tested on a cell line the model never saw

Leave-one-cell-line-out: signatures, feature selection and models are built on three lines and scored on the fourth. Simple baselines run next to complex ones, and every hypothesis below was written before the comparison was run.

Why not split by cells? Replicates from one line share almost everything. Testing on them flatters the model. A whole new cell line is a fairer exam.

09 / The mathematics of switching

Why a reversible state is enough to cause relapse

A two-state linear model: sensitive cells S and persisters P, with switching rates that change when the drug is on. The long-run fate is set by the largest eigenvalue of the rate matrix. This panel is theory with illustrative rates, checked against an independent Octave implementation in CI.

Limitations

Four cell lines, three replicates each. Long washouts differ in length between lines. Bulk data cannot resolve subpopulations. Regulator activity is inferred from expression of targets, so it is not independent of expression. No new wet-lab experiments and no clinical validation. Literature records were curated by hand and can be incomplete.

Next datasets

Single-cell: GSE243562 (patient-derived xenografts, residual tumour) and the PC9 osimertinib arm of GSE275199, kept separate from its H358 sotorasib arm. Chromatin: GSE193259 ATAC-seq. These are listed as planned, not run.

Prior work we build on

PersistTrace is a computational research framework for EGFR-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer cell models treated with osimertinib. It separates acute drug responses from persistent molecular changes and keeps observed markers apart from candidate mechanisms. It does not diagnose patients, recommend treatment or replace laboratory validation.

Data: NCBI GEO GSE193258 (Criscione et al., npj Precision Oncology 2022). Gene sets: MSigDB hallmark. Regulons: CollecTRI. Built by PRITIDIPADAS for a science fair. Source code.
