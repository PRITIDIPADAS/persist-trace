A computational study of drug-tolerant persister cells · EGFR-mutant lung cancer · osimertinib

The tumour shrinks.
Some cancer cells survive.
What are they becoming?

Lung cancer cells can outlast osimertinib without a single new mutation. They change state. PersistTrace reads 60 public RNA-seq experiments, removes the part that is only day-one drug stress, measures how long the cells remember the drug, and then checks its answers in single cells and in patient-derived tumours.

Pritidipa Das

Index of plates

    Plate I

    Two and a half million people a year, and a drug that works until it doesn't

    Lung cancer is the most common cancer and the deadliest. About a third of non-small-cell cases carry an EGFR mutation. Osimertinib shrinks most of these tumours, yet in the FLAURA trial the median time to progression was 18.9 months. The cells that survive the first months are where relapse starts.

    Who is affected. People with EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma, many of whom never smoked. EGFR mutations are found more often in women (43.7%) than men (24.0%), and in non-smokers (49.3%).

    Who would use this. Cancer biology and translational labs deciding which persister genes to test next. Today they compare treated with untreated cells and pick the biggest fold changes, which mixes drug stress with the persister state.

    Plate II

    The drug locks onto EGFR. The persister survives anyway.

    Osimertinib binds the ATP pocket of EGFR and is irreversible: its acrylamide tail reacts with cysteine 797. Mutations at that cysteine (C797S) are one known escape route. Persisters use no mutation at all, which is why they need a different kind of analysis.

    Why a non-genetic problem needs AI. There is no mutation to sequence. The signal is spread across thousands of genes, mixed with stress, and changes over weeks. Separating it needs statistical models across many samples, not a single lab test.

    Plate III

    Five moments in a cancer cell's encounter with the drug

    The primary data are 60 RNA-seq samples from Criscione et al. (2022): four EGFR-mutant cell lines, three replicates, five conditions. The same design lets us ask what is stress, what is persistence and what fades.

    How a researcher uses it

    1. Load the public data. One command downloads GEO files and records their checksums.
    2. Separate stress from persistence for every gene in every line.
    3. Follow each change into the washout and measure its memory.
    4. Check the result in single cells and patient-derived tumours.
    5. Read the passport before choosing which gene to test in the lab.

    Plate V

    Subtract the stress. Keep the persister.

    For each gene the persister change P is split into what the 24-hour response predicts, β·A, and what it does not, S. One robust slope per cell line, then a random-effects meta-analysis across the four lines.

    Plate VI

    The cells remember the drug for weeks

    Memory is the share of a persister change still present after washout. Where the regrowth day is known, an exponential fit gives a half-life. These are population averages: a return to baseline fits reversal, and it also fits other cells outgrowing the persisters.

    Withdrawal classes, reproducible in 3 of 4 lines

    Plate VII

    Asleep, or quietly copying DNA?

    Persisters are often called dormant. In bulk data, proliferation genes crash after 24 hours and then partly come back while the drug is still on. A 2026 dataset of PC9 persisters sorted by cell-cycle phase lets us ask whether the cycling ones carry the persister programme too.

    Programmes shifted beyond stress

    Plate VIII

    Beyond the dish average: single cells and real tumours

    The signatures were frozen from the four cell lines, then scored in two datasets they had never seen: PC9 single cells under osimertinib (GSE275199) and tumour cells from patient-derived xenografts before and after six weeks of treatment (GSE243562).

    Plate IX

    Every line on this graph says where it came from

    Genes, regulators, programmes and published studies. Solid lines were measured or reported, dashed lines were inferred. A computer guess and a lab experiment never look the same. Click a gene to open its passport.

    Plate X

    Marker or mechanism? Six separate answers and no secret score

    The label at the top is the strongest claim the evidence allows, chosen by written rules. Contradictions are listed, not averaged away.

    Start with TROP2. A 2025 knockout study found it was not needed for persister formation. A 2026 study reported that it helps persisters survive. Both can be true in different set-ups.

    Plate XI

    Tested on a cell line the model never saw

    Signatures and models are built on three lines and scored on the fourth. Hypotheses H1 to H6 were committed to GitHub before the first real run; later additions are marked post hoc. Failures stay on the page.

    Plate XII

    Why a reversible state is enough for relapse

    Two populations, sensitive S and persister P, switching at rates that depend on the drug. The largest eigenvalue of the rate matrix decides whether the tumour grows. The rates are illustrative; the same model runs in Python, JavaScript and Octave, and CI checks that Python and Octave agree.
